**Review of the intended fix, before the hunk is frozen.** I read the reset path end to end. The feed-style call is the right mechanism and the smallest one:

- **Placement.** Immediately after the three subscribes in init, before `recover_lifecycle`. Subscribe first, reset second, exactly as feed does. A client's reconnect after the reset then lands in the new owner's mailbox. The reverse order can lose a resync on a fresh stream.
- **Scope of effect.** The broadcast reaches every local connection owner. Each link resets only if it is inbound and on the directory channel, replies `channel_owner_restarted` to any queued reliable send, and exits. Outbound links, other channels and the connections themselves are untouched. Directory sends are best effort, so no caller sees that error.
- **Client-side recovery already exists.** The client's pinned outbound link dies on stream reset, its monitor fires, `handle_control_link_down` redials through the existing dial queue, `install_control_link` sends the snapshot request, and the new root process retains the subscriber. If the resync arrives before root's first proof, the pre-proof retention path holds it until the proof lands.
- **Root's own upstream side.** The restarted process rebuilds its outbound control links through the normal dial path, so multi-root subscriptions also recover.
- **Message loss window.** A resync sent on an old stream between the old owner's death and the new subscribe is published to no subscriber and dropped. The reset then kills that stream and the client resends on reconnect. No permanent loss.
- **Not needed.** No change to the directory ETS owner restart path, which already clears generations and resends upstream resyncs while the control process is alive.
