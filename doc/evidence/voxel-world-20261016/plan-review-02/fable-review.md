**Verdict: approve with required corrections.** The architecture holds: one fixed terrain body, Prolog-owned field and policy, residency as a cache rather than authored structure, and digging through the existing signed transaction. ## Blockers: contracts to pin before implementation

**B1. The coverage contract does not exist yet, and nothing in the native owner approximates it.** `lib.rs:156` clears `query_ready` only between an install and the next step; after that step, `motion.rs:144-240` treats whatever colliders exist as the whole world. A controlled agent walking into a chunk that was never materialized gets `Clear`, and a dynamic crate over an evicted chunk falls until `sample()` at `lib.rs:457` trips `coordinate_allowance`, which `quod_physics.erl:665` turns into a failed world. Section 9.1.5 states the goal correctly but not the rule. Pin, in the plan: the owner computes a conservative envelope per step from every non-fixed body's pose plus one step of velocity, gravity and skin, plus every pending drive, teleport path and drop column, plus every outstanding query sweep; the step, query or input proceeds only if every chunk cell intersecting that envelope within the world's vertical envelope is resident at the installed terrain revision and the broad phase has completed a step since the last collider change. A chunk counts as resident only when a materialized result exists for it, including the empty and fully-solid results; absence is never resident. When the gate fails, the owner cancels the step timer exactly as `pause_steps` does at `quod_physics.erl:424`, requests the missing cells, and resumes on the installed notice. This is a fixable planning omission, not an implementation detail, because it decides what "unavailable" means for every later slice.

**B2. Triangle delivery is ambiguous in section 5, and one reading breaks two invariants.** The marks list is produced inside the signed read by `client_world_view` at `common_predicates.pl:9-22` and re-proven on every refresh; every socket reply is bounded by the max-reply constant at `quod_client_view.erl:655` and closes the socket at 1009 when exceeded. If vertices ride inside the mark, every dig re-triangulates the whole visible set inside a proof worker and the payload hits a genuine codec limit. Pin instead: the mark carries only the anchored definition reference, chunk revision set, kernel version and a descriptor digest, validated by a new clause in `quod_rendering.pl` beside `geometry/3`; the materialized vertices are owner-retained immutable results keyed by input digest and pushed over the same authorized socket as a new message kind, each frame under the existing reply envelope, exactly as `physics_frame` is pushed at `quod_client_view.erl:542`. The browser decodes it in `marks.js` under the existing kind table and leases it through `render-resources.js` by digest. State the packing in the spike so the per-frame bound is met by construction.

**B3. Quantization degeneracy will fail the whole world unless the mesher is total.** `geometry.rs:139-141` rejects any zero-area triangle with `InvalidBody`, and `quod_physics.erl:361` retires the world on that error. Marching cubes at integer-millimetre output places a vertex on a lattice node whenever the crossing parameter rounds to 0 or 1; with sample spacings of hundreds of millimetres this happens on a measurable fraction of edges, so rejecting the chunk, as section 7 proposes, makes ordinary terrain frequently unavailable. Pin a canonical welding rule in the spike: a crossing that quantizes onto a node becomes that node's shared vertex, adjacent cells compute the same vertex by construction, and triangles with two equal indices are dropped on both sides so the surface stays closed. With that rule the mesher never produces invalid native input, so a dig proof needs no triangulation to validate, and "unavailable region" arises only from resource refusal.

## Required clarifications

**R1. The terrain body row handed to the native must be stable across digs.** `lib.rs:127-129` rebuilds a body wholesale when its row differs, and `quod_physics.erl:303-321` sends the complete body list. If the terrain row embeds an override digest, every dig drops every resident subcollider. State that the owned shape term references the world definition without override state, that the structure revision alone carries the change, and that chunk replacement goes through a new per-chunk native entry under the existing body, with the Erlang owner keeping chunk-to-handle maps. Also state that the per-install geometry budget at `lib.rs:120` does not cover subcolliders, so Prolog supplies a separate residency allowance for resident vertices, triangles and chunk count.

**R2. Contact allowance multiplicity.** `lib.rs:468-475` counts contact pairs per collider and fails the world when the count reaches the allowance. A capsule straddling four chunk meshes consumes four pairs where one body pair was budgeted. Either the policy budget is defined per collider pair with chunk fan-out included, or the native coalesces pairs per body before counting. Choose and record it.

**R3. Coordinate allowance is applied to absolute world coordinates in Prolog today.** `physics_valid_body` at `quod_physics.pl:615-620`, teleport and reposition checks at lines 452-454, rest measurements at line 830 and the native envelope at `lib.rs:457` all compare absolute positions to one `CoordinateMm`. Unbounded worlds need that split: an authored domain envelope in Prolog with exact integers, and a native envelope relative to a run origin. Enumerate every seam above and say which frame it operates in after the change. I recommend a per-run integer origin fixed at activation and carried in the fence and frame schema, with the Erlang owner converting at its boundary and an origin change treated as an explicit run-generation boundary rather than a live rebase of Rapier bodies; the plan's invariants in 9.1.8 hold either way, but the live rebase is a larger native surface than the accepted scope requires. Float32 jitter in Babylon is a client rendering concern and does not need a protocol change beyond the origin tag.

**R4. Define which checkpoints a dig invalidates.** Advancing `physics_structure` fences pending samples through `physics_valid_sample` at `quod_physics.pl:686-695` but touches no committed `physics_checkpoint` row; `restore_rest` at `lib.rs:182-199` accepts a floating pose because it only rejects overlap. Either state that terrain edits leave free-body checkpoints alone and the body falls under gravity after restore, or remove checkpoints of bodies whose envelope intersects changed chunks. Section 6 step 4 currently says "relevant" without defining it.

**R5. Surfaces cannot detect interior solid.** Parry trimesh contact reports distance to triangles, so a body authored or repositioned inside rock reads `Clear` at `motion.rs:176-179` and then cannot exit. The plan already defers fill. Add that creation validates spawn poses against the canonical field, not the mesh, and that reposition and teleport destinations whose sample is solid are refused by the owner using the deterministic field.

**R6. Separate the transaction encoding envelope from the patch policy budget.** A brush closure commits replacement patches for every affected chunk and halo; the canonical transaction bound is a codec constraint, not policy. Pick a chunk size such that the maximal policy brush fits one entry, and report which bound was exceeded. I could not verify the exact constants because the limits header is not in the frozen set.

**R7. State the per-edit cost honestly.** Every commit above a view's read height triggers `refresh(durable_gap)` at `quod_client_view.erl:623`, and every commit touching the physics basis suspends inputs through `physics_invalidated` at `quod_physics.erl:159-163` and `quod_runtime.erl:971-977`. In a shared world every dig by anyone re-signs every viewer's read and suspends their controls until reinstall. Section 7 says this for physics; say it for views too, and make it a measured acceptance item.

**R8. Where the primitive runs and how it is classified.** The resource selector runs under a policy-verdict context at `quod_runtime.erl:2918-2919` with a basis that fences any unknown native call to `parent` at `quod_resource_basis.erl:46-49`, which would reselect on every commit. State that `physics_projection/2` only enumerates descriptors and that materialization happens in the retained Erlang owner, and that any primitive callable from Prolog is added to the pure list at `quod_resource_basis.erl:78-79`. Also note that `quod_common_primitives` is release-coupled across all validators rather than a founded manifest module, which section 10 should name as the installation path.

**R9. Slice 2 must include the coverage gate.** A fully resident bounded fixture makes the gate trivially true, but shipping constituent surfaces without it leaves a path where absent equals air until slice 5. Move the gate into slice 2.

## Scope, optional improvements and verification

The accepted scope is sufficient. The three extension items cover the pure primitive, chunk colliders under the existing owner, and movement-scoped updates on the authorized view connection; the socket geometry push in B2 and the per-chunk native entry in R1 are the natural counterparts of those items, not new capabilities. No second engine, broker or queue appears.

Optional improvements, each a reuse point rather than a defect: drop `voxel_terrain_binding/3`, because `physics_body_definition/6` already holds the body identity and revision and a second fact is a second mutable truth. Derive the interest centre from the server-known controlled body pose and drive direction so the client hint is needed only for observer views. Commit the output digest with each dig so the materializer on every node checks determinism against committed evidence. Use `transaction/1` as the other physics transitions do rather than forcing a brush into a target-state `goal/1` formulation.

Section 9.3 is compatible as written: the coverage pin in B1 must include foreign-sourced live bodies admitted through `physics_space_change`, residency selection must be three-dimensional for elevated viewpoints, and the one-viewer profile at `quod_physics.erl:78` means the lobby occupant is the single viewer. Aggregation prerequisites in 9.2 are adequate.

Verification obligations for implementation: prove B1 through the real owner with a dynamic body over an evicted chunk and a controlled travel into an unmaterialized cell, both synchronized on installed notices; prove B3 with fixtures that force crossings within one quantum of a node across chunk boundaries; count native installs, contact pairs and reply bytes per dig through `status` counters; and run the standing sequential gates at the release boundary.

Uncertainties: I did not run any tests or builds. The Vega et al. corrections, FastNoiseLite portability and Parry interior-detection behaviour are external knowledge, not verified in this tree. The reply and request byte constants, Rapier multi-collider semantics beyond the `insert_with_parent` call at `lib.rs:151`, and the Excalidraw were not inspected.
