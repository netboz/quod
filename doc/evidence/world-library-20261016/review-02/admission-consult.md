## Consult: validator-side material admission across the three write lanes

Scope: current source in `/home/yan/src/quod` (read only). Of the files cited, only `quod_material_admission.erl`, `quod_dtx.erl` and `quod_selection_basis.erl` differ from the review-02 freeze; every other seam below is identical in both trees.

### 1. Where fresh relayed material becomes VM atoms today

The only allocation primitive is `quod_wire_term:materialize_symbols/1` (`src/quod_wire_term.erl:200`). In wrapped mode a symbol that the VM already knows decodes to that atom and only unknown names stay opaque (`src/quod_wire_term.erl:719-724`), so wrapped records keep working for everything that inspects known functors. Who calls the primitive, per lane:

**Lane 1, one writer.** Two seams, both before any validation runs.
- Validators receive a proposal: `quod_relay.erl:56-57` calls `quod_ledger:decode_block(BlockBytes)`, whose one-argument form defaults to `materialized` (`src/quod_ledger.erl:198-199`), so `decode_payload` materializes every transaction (`src/quod_ledger.erl:286-297`, through `quod_transaction.erl:1279-1290` and `materialize_decoded/2` at `:1889-1891`). That `#block{}` is what `preflight_proposal` keeps, what `content/4` validates in check mode (`src/quod_prolog.erl:7461-7465`), and what becomes the committed entry (`src/quod_simplex.erl:552`) later applied in claim mode (`src/quod_committed_projection.erl:164-168`).
- The leader receives a relayed submission: `src/quod_simplex.erl:13203` and `:14527-14533` call the two-argument `quod_transaction:decode_verified_submission/2`, which hard-codes `materialized` (`src/quod_transaction.erl:1331-1340`).

**Lane 2, atomic group.** Plans travel as blobs and stay opaque through `quod_atomic:decode_material` and `quod_dtx:decode`. Allocation happens in exactly one place, `quod_commit_validation:materialize_prepared_plan/1` (`src/quod_commit_validation.erl:758-759`), reached from `own_vote_choice` (`:668-676`), `validate_prepared_plan` (`:735-743`), `prepared_material` (`:206-211`) and `read_only_plan` (`:187-204`, the endpoint's `read_attest` at `src/quod_simplex.erl:6356-6358`). This lane already defers allocation to the validation owner. It lacks only the check.

**Lane 3, independent operation.** The claim arrives wrapped (`quod_transaction:decode_evidence/1`, `src/quod_transaction.erl:333-354`). Allocation happens in `remote_application_material/3` at `src/quod_transaction.erl:197` (`quod_dtx:material(Plan)`), reached from `remote_application_checked` (`src/quod_commit_validation.erl:454-456`) in both check mode (`:221-228`) and claim mode (`src/quod_committed_projection.erl:270-285`), and from the endpoint's `decode_claimed_application` (`src/quod_simplex.erl:6436-6447`, via `remote_application/3` at `src/quod_transaction.erl:170-174`) before `submit_role` runs any validation.

**A fourth fresh seam outside the lanes.** `quod_relay.erl:64-66` decodes `certified_block_bytes` materialized before `ingest_certified_block` checks the persisted certificate, so an admitted peer can allocate atoms on an honest node with bytes that never verify.

**Paths that must stay policy free and already are.** Own ledger replay opens materialized (`src/quod_ledger_store.erl:161`). Catch-up decodes wrapped (`src/quod_catchup.erl:454`, `:780`, `:880`), verifies certificates (`:747`, `:993`) and only then materializes the verified range (`:1038`). Foreign caches, the recovery endpoint and the current-view module decode only. Own sealed plans (`src/quod_prolog.erl:5120`, `:6256`, `src/quod_scope_session.erl:971`, effect journal) and admitted founding input (`src/quod_ontology.erl:832`) are local. The catch-up pattern, verify wrapped then materialize, is the existing precedent the three fresh seams should follow.

### 2. Smallest coherent correction, existing owners only

Make `quod_commit_validation` the one place where fresh material becomes atoms, keyed by its existing `mode()`. Its module doc already states that the mode "selects whether admission policy is being checked before a vote or a certified record is being projected" (`src/quod_commit_validation.erl:9-12`). Check mode admits, then materializes. Claim mode materializes without policy, which keeps certified history independent of today's policy by construction.

Concretely:
- `quod_relay.erl:57` and `:66` decode with `wrapped`. `src/quod_simplex.erl:14530` passes `wrapped` through the existing four-argument `decode_verified_submission/4` (`src/quod_transaction.erl:1344`). No bytes, identities or signatures change; `valid_block_view` already re-encodes wrapped (`src/quod_ledger.erl:381`), signatures verify over canonical bytes, and functor checks go through `callable_functor`, which accepts opaque symbols (`src/quod_wire_term.erl:53-66`).
- Lane 2 and lane 3 share one measurement. `admit_core` already decodes the material, counts symbols and reads `diff_ops` and `read_functors` (`src/quod_dtx.erl:309-333`). Extract that into `quod_dtx:admitted_material(Plan, Limits)` returning `{ok, Material}` only after `quod_material_admission:check/3`, and call it from `admit_core`, from `materialize_prepared_plan` in check mode, and from `remote_application_material` in check mode. Seal and validation then agree byte for byte, so an honest proposer can never be refused by an honest validator except when the policy itself changed, which OCC on the sealed policy reads rejects anyway.
- Lane 1 has no plan on the wire, so validators measure the wrapped `{Diff, ReadCheck, Effects}` tuple with `quod_material_admission:usage/1` plus `operations` and `reads` counts. That is a subset of the proposer's plan measurement (the transcript is absent), so it is never stricter than the seal; it still bounds what a faulty proposer can make an honest node allocate.
- The policy source on validators is `policy(plan, Context#context.est)`, the same frozen parent the OCC check uses (`validate_prepared_occ`, `:776-790`). It is a Prolog read over already-known atoms, so it runs before any allocation.
- Refusals use the existing per-lane shapes: `{refused, [{material_limit, ...}]}` for a vote (`own_vote_choice`, `:694-697`), `{reject, Public}` for a remote application by adding the two material reasons to `remote_rejection_reason` (`:509-516`), so the durable claim still receives its B outcome as `:229-231` requires, and `{invalid, Reason}` for lane 1 content in check mode, next to the manifest check in `continue_ordinary_content_validation` (`:331-336`). The public error vocabulary already exists in `quod_scope_wire:validate_public_error`.

Do not put the check inside `materialize_symbols`. The codec has no committed state and no notion of fresh versus certified; the relay message kind and the validation mode are where that knowledge lives.

One obligation to verify during implementation: the leader's ingress validation hook (`ingress_route_request`, `src/quod_simplex.erl` after `:14560`) must materialize a wrapped relayed change through the same check-mode path before block inclusion. I did not trace whether `route_change_acceptable` reaches `content/4`.

### 3. Self-lock: malformed policy versus deliberate zero

The codebase already draws this exact line for the entry policy. An owner may write a `can_invoke/4` clause that denies everyone; that is semantic self-denial and allowed. An owner may not remove the predicate: `quod_diff:apply_ops_preserving_policy_report` refuses a diff that leaves no `can_invoke/4` clause with `policy_self_seal_forbidden` (`src/quod_diff.erl:149-158`, `:353-358`), validators enforce it in `validate_prepared_candidate` (`src/quod_commit_validation.erl:792-798`), and certified apply records it as a rejected outcome (`src/quod_committed_projection.erl:298-306`). Structural self-seal is an invariant; semantic self-denial is a right.

Map material policy onto that line:
- A zero allowance is semantic. `valid_limits` accepts zero (`src/quod_material_admission.erl`, `valid_limits/2`). For the `plan` kind it freezes the ontology permanently, because every plan has at least one symbol and one operation; that is the owner's call, exactly like a denying `can_invoke` clause. Do not add a recovery budget. For the `founding` kind in root it freezes creation while root's own plan lane stays writable, so it is reversible. Say both plainly in `material_policy.pl`.
- A malformed policy is structural: a duplicate, unknown or negative row, a non-integer that is not `unlimited`, or a missing resource. Admission cannot select limits, and the correcting write is itself refused, which is the lockout I reported. The live `validate_update/4` (`src/quod_material_admission.erl:21-33`) now refuses such a write at the proposer's seal (`src/quod_dtx.erl:198-208`). That protects honest proposers but not honest nodes against a faulty proposer. Put the same selector check where `policy_self_seal_forbidden` lives: after `apply_ops_preserving_policy_report` in `validate_prepared_candidate` (validators) and in `apply_ordinary_transaction` (certified apply, `src/quod_committed_projection.erl:290-306`), when the diff touches `material_limit_override/3` or the default functors. Keep `invalid_material_policy` as its own reason, distinct from `{material_limit, ...}`, and add it to `remote_rejection_reason`. Running the selector from `quod_diff` itself would pull a proof session into a pure diff module; two call sites of the existing `validate_update/4` keep one implementation without that dependency.

### 4. Does this need an architectural decision?

Mostly no. Validator-side admission before allocation is the same rule the authorized refactor states for founding ("raw-symbol refusal before atom allocation") applied to the remaining fresh seams, through owners the refactor already named: the relay's decode mode, `quod_commit_validation`'s existing mode, `quod_dtx`'s existing measurement and `quod_material_admission`. No new lane, owner, transport, format or constant. The malformed-policy invariant at validators and apply reuses the `policy_self_seal_forbidden` pattern.

Two points need Yan's explicit word, because they change what consensus refuses:
- Over-policy fresh material becomes a refused vote, a public reject, or an invalid lane-1 proposal. For lane 1 only, a policy tightened between the proposer's seal and validation now costs the leader an invalid proposal and a round instead of an ordinary rejected outcome at apply. The alternative, deciding it at apply against the committed parent's policy, is deterministic but means apply consults policy, which the brief rules out. I recommend check-mode refusal and accepting the rare race cost.
- Lane 1 validators measure a subset of what the proposer measured. This is deliberate and conservative, but it is an asymmetry worth recording in `doc/multiwrite-architecture.md` next to the existing admission paragraph.

Test obligations if adopted: per lane, a proposal or vote carrying material over the target's policy is refused and the node's atom table does not grow (assert `binary_to_existing_atom` still fails afterwards); the same record, constructed as certified, still applies; wrapped proposals pass `well_formed_block`, signature, manifest and membership checks unchanged; a malformed override is rejected at seal, at vote and at certified apply with `invalid_material_policy`, while a zero override is admitted and then refuses the next plan with `{material_limit, ...}`.
