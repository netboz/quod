## 1. Labels turn with the head

Cause: `ScenePanel.Install` builds each label as a `Label3D` with
`Billboard=Enabled` (`client/godot/ScenePanel.cs:287`). Godot then turns the
label to the camera every frame, head roll included.

Fix: no billboard. The label keeps the orientation of its thing: it faces the
thing's front. A space created "Near my agent" takes the agent's rotation, so
its front faces where its creator stood. Seen from behind, a `Label3D` shows
mirrored text, so a second copy of the same text faces the back (decided).

The ray-tip label (`OpenXRInput.rayLabel`) is billboarded too and repeats the
name already written above the pointed thing. It is removed (decided), with
`PointTarget`'s label code; the pointed thing keeps its lit look.

## 2. Pie on a pointed space, Create space, no form

**Reproduced (2026-11-14, Phase 1).** With Yan's gesture, the pad's release
frame takes the wedge away from the ray. The pad pressed near its centre and
held, the ray brought onto a wedge, the pad released: if the thumb's last
sample differs from the one before by 0.01 or more (it shifted while lifting,
or lifted off and reads 0,0), `ControllerInput.Frame` passes that sample to the
pie (`MoveMenu`) before it commits (`CommitMenu`). A thumb near the centre
points nothing, so the pie cancels. Evidence: `p1-repro-01.log` and
`p1-repro-02.log` in `doc/evidence/vr-retest-20261114/` (both lifts fail with
`pie ended cancelled: released on no wedge … steered_by=pad`); the check is
kept as `p1-repro-check.patch` and goes in with the fix.

Reading the session's records again: it is not about a pointed thing. Pies of
the current space ended the same way at 12:38:29, 12:39:14 and 12:39:22; pies
of pointed spaces worked at 12:36:11, 12:37:56 and 12:38:07. Where a "press
pie" line appears, the trigger chose the wedge, and the trigger never failed.
A pad release depends on how the thumb leaves the pad, so it works or fails
by chance.

Proposed fix, for Yan's decision: the sample on which the pad comes up is
lifting, not choosing. On that frame the router commits without passing its
axes; the wedge lit just before is chosen (`ControllerInput.Frame`, one line).
A thumb that rests at the centre before the release still cancels, as today.
The ray/thumb fight while the pad is held stays as it is.

What follows was written before the reproduction.

What the records show:

- 12:39:37 and 12:39:43 (UTC): pies opened on "Foamstoke" and "Gidgog", each
  answered and drawn in 1.4 s. After each, the node received nothing. A
  chosen wedge always starts two reads (`eidolons`, `gui_operations`); none
  came. No trigger press on the pie was logged. The next event is another pie.
- 12:36:11: a pie on a pointed space did bring the creation form (reads at
  12:36:16, Create pressed at 12:36:23).

So in the two failing cases no wedge was committed; the form code was never
reached. In the code, the path from a committed "Create space" wedge to the
form is the same with or without a pointed thing (`Main.Activate`,
`EidolonPanel.OpenChoice`): the pointed thing is only compared with the
wedge's subject, and "Create space" belongs to the class, so the form goes to
the personal panel.

What can end a pie with nothing, in the code:

- The pad is released while no wedge is lit: the pie cancels
  (`ControllerInput.Frame` → `CommitMenu` → `PieNavigator.Commit`).
- The ray and the thumb both steer the pie and overwrite each other
  (`PieMenu.PointerAt`, `MoveAxes`); the last one to move decides at release.
  Known since 2026-11-13, left alone on Yan's word.
- "Create here" and, inside it, "Create space" are both the top wedge. After
  a sub-pie opens, the direction that was held points nothing until it moves
  away (`PieNavigator.rearm`). In VR the sub-pie can shift only 76 px, so the
  ray is still near its hub.

None of these depends on a pointed thing, so none explains why it fails there
and works on the space's own pie. One difference exists: the pie of a pointed
thing opens centred on the ray; the space's pie is lowered below it. Whether
that matters is not shown.

Yan's gesture (answered 2026-11-14): pad held, ray on the wedge, pad
released. That points at the release itself. On the frame the pad comes up,
`ControllerInput.Frame` first passes the thumb's position to the pie
(`MoveMenu`) and only then commits (`CommitMenu`). If the thumb shifted while
lifting, the thumb's direction replaces the wedge the ray had lit: a thumb
near the pad's centre lights nothing and the pie cancels; a thumb elsewhere
picks another wedge. This is the leading suspect, not a proven cause: it does
not explain either why the space's own pie worked four times with the same
gesture.

Phase 1 reproduces it before anything is changed, and the client writes how
each pie ended.

## 5. The strip: laggy and too high

Where it is: 1.5 m away, 25° below eye level, 1.1 m wide
(`OpenXRInput.PlaceHud`, numbers in `DeviceProfile.Vive`).

Cause of the lag (Yan: it stays behind when he turns, then jumps): the
strip's heading keeps its value until the head has turned more than 35°, then
takes the head's heading at once (`DeviceProfile.FollowYaw`, called from
`PlaceHud`).

A second fault, same function: its place is computed from the head's position
on every frame, so it is glued to the head's movement.

Fix (decided): the strip is put in the room like the panel and stays there.
It takes a new place in front only when the head has turned past the follow
angle or moved 0.5 m, in one glide of 0.3 s. This is rule 5 of the research
note (never head-locked). The follow angle stays at Yan's 35°; with a strip
41° wide, half of it is out of view by then, so that number may need to come
down after the next test.

Height (decided): 35° below eye level. The panel keeps its present place
(centre 9° below); its angle becomes its own profile value, no longer derived
from the strip's.

## 7. The pie delay: the slow step

It is the node's proof of `client_menu`. Everything else is small.

| Step | Pointed space's pie | Current space, three child spaces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client opens the socket | 19 ms | 13 ms |
| Node checks session, signatures, target | under 2 ms | under 2 ms |
| **Node proves `client_menu`** | **1,330 ms** | **3,291 ms** |
| Node subscribes and answers | 1 ms | 1 ms |
| Answer reaches the client and is decoded | about 24 ms | about 21 ms |
| Client draws | 17 ms | 14 ms |
| Icons, first time only, after drawing | 18–48 ms each | |

Inside the proof: 615 calls into other ontologies for the first pie, 513 of
them into `quod:space`; 1,440 for the second, 1,200 into `quod:space`. Each
is its own small proof, about 2 ms. Each child space listed under "Go to"
adds about 275 calls and 0.65 s: 2.0 s with one child, 2.6 with two, 3.3 with
three, 4.5 to 5.2 s in the first space with five or six.

Why, from the code: the user's ontology holds the instances; every class rule
is in `quod:space` and is called across. `space_menu` reads
`gui_menu_source` three times (the space, the class, the storage). For "Go
to", `space_menu_destination` reads the whole `gui_menu_source` of every
child space and keeps one item, its Enter.

Not known yet:

- Which goals make up the 500 calls. The spans carry the ontology called, not
  the goal.
- What the tracing costs. At 1 in 1 the node writes 16,848 spans for a 1.3 s
  pie. Pies over 4 s are missing from Tempo; my guess is that such a trace is
  too large to be kept.

Phase 5 adds the goal's name to the span, times one pie with tracing at
1 in 20, and brings back the fix. First candidate: "Go to" reads each child's
Enter offer alone.

**Found (2026-11-14, local).** The same pie in the local fixture makes 617
cross-ontology calls, as on the live network (615). Two thirds of them, 418,
are one goal: `isa/2` asked of `quod:space`. One child space under "Go to"
adds 248 calls, 181 of them `isa/2`. Evidence:
`p5-pie-goals-local.txt`, from the scratch script `p5-pie-goals-scratch.erl`
(an Erlang call trace of `quod_ask:open/5` around one signed `client_menu`
read; it is not part of the test suite).

Why: the user's ontology holds the instance `room` of the class
`quod:space:space`. Every `isa(quod:space:space, C)` there is answered by
asking `quod:space` (`'$quod_isa_path'/3` in `common_predicates.pl`): one
remote proof per call. `class_eidolons/4` makes those calls in a loop: once
per class to collect candidates, then, to keep only the most specific
recipe, `isa(Other,Class), \+ isa(Class,Other)` for every pair of candidates.
Each `gui_menu_source/2` read of a space runs `entity_eidolons/4` again, and
the pie reads it four times plus once per child.

Proposed fix, for Yan's decision: `class_eidolons/4` reads each class's
ancestors once (one `findall` over `isa/2`, one remote ask per class) and
answers the "more specific than" test by membership in those lists, as
`gui_class_chain/3` already does in `quod_gui.pl` since 0.7.281. Expected:
the 418 `isa/2` calls become a handful; no menu changes. Then "Go to" reading
only each child's Enter offer, if still needed.

**Built (Yan: build now, release later).** `class_eidolons/4` reads each
class's ancestors once (`class_ancestors/2`, canonical names) and compares
the lists (`class_below/3`). The same local pie, measured the same way
(`p5-pie-goals-local-after-fix.txt`):

| Pie of the current space | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No child under Go to | 617 calls, 418 of them `isa/2`; 5,762 ms | 216 calls, 46 `isa/2`; 440 ms |
| One child | 865 calls, 599 `isa/2`; 9,061 ms | 288 calls, 65 `isa/2`; 503 ms |

Times are local and include the call trace's own cost; they compare, they do
not predict the live numbers. The menu itself must not change: the full EUnit
suite passes, 4362 tests, 0 failures.

Release cost: `common_predicates.pl` loads from the node image, so this is a
node image change; per Yan's ruling of 2026-11-08 a baked-file change is a
