# VR forms, panel material, object spawn and bounds — research digest (2026-11-11)

Follow-up to `vr-desktop-ergonomics-20261105.md`; nothing from that file is repeated here.
Vendor pages first. (2nd) = secondary, community or 2D-web source. (computed) = my arithmetic
from the vendor's numbers. "No number" = the page gives only qualitative guidance.

## 1. Panel material and translucency
1. **Apple says translucent, not opaque.** visionOS windows use a system "glass" that lets the surroundings show through; "Prefer translucency to opaque colors in windows", opaque areas "block people's view" and feel constricting. Glass adapts to what is behind it; no opacity number is published. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials
2. **But keep a backplate.** Removing the glass makes text "less legible" and elements look unrelated; a fully opaque one feels "constricting and heavy". https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows
3. **Inner parts get their own material.** Lighter (thin) for buttons and selected items; regular to separate sections; darker (thick) for a dark element. Use darker material behind input fields to raise contrast; never stack light on light. https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/
4. **Text on glass.** White text by default, heavier weights (body medium, titles bold), three brightness levels (label, secondary, tertiary — tertiary only where legibility does not matter). Avoid white-background buttons unless selected. (same URL)
5. **Apple's only dimming number:** over bright content put a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity behind clear glass; use the blurring "regular" variant for text-heavy parts (alerts, sidebars, popovers). Stated for Liquid Glass on all platforms, not visionOS only. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials
6. **Microsoft says opaque (HoloLens).** "Avoid transparent backplate": legibility drops on complex backgrounds, and overlapping translucent things confuse depth. Accepted compromise: a translucent control in front of an opaque backplate. Dark backgrounds, semibold or bold text. Part of the reasoning is specific to see-through displays. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/button and https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/designing-content-for-holographic-display
7. **Meta: no opacity or blur number.** Its Layout SDK windows "do not support transparency" (opaque root required). Contrast 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and non-text parts; avoid pure #FFFFFF and #000000; "err on the side of higher contrast". https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/styles_color/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/multi-window-layouts/
8. **Blur and darken behind.** Meta: blurring the background behind an opened menu is comfortable; theater view darkens the surroundings (no amount given). https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/display/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/panels/
9. **Modal dimming.** visionOS: a sheet makes the parent window "push back and dim"; a second sheet adds another dimming layer (no percentage). Android XR: a dialog appears at the panel's depth and the panel is pushed back 125dp. https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10076/ and https://developer.android.com/develop/xr/jetpack-xr-sdk/compose/spatial-dialog
10. **Measured (desktop, peer-reviewed).** Text menus up to 50% transparent performed like opaque ones; 75% was worse, 90–100% worst; text and wireframe backgrounds interfere most, solid images least. Harrison & Vicente, CHI 1996. https://www.chi1996.chistatic.hosting.acm.org/proceedings/papers/Harrison/blh_txt.html

## 2. Form layout
11. **Type as little as possible.** Take data from the system, prefill defaults, "offer choices instead of requiring text entry" (picker, menu). https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data
12. **One column.** "Stack multiple text fields vertically", consistent widths, even spacing, a label per field plus an optional hint inside. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields — 2D-web studies agree: single column, label directly above, radio buttons for 2–3 options, no placeholder-as-label (2nd). https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/
13. **Field count per VR panel:** no vendor number. Guidance is only "keep it short" (2nd, NN/g) and "keep the main controls in a single UI panel". https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/comfort/
14. **Target size, visionOS.** Buttons 28/32/44/52/64 pt (44 regular); every target needs a 60 pt area; 16 pt between stacked buttons. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons and WWDC23 10076 (URL above)
15. **Target size, Meta.** Hit target 48dp minimum, 60dp for primary controls, visible part at least 32dp; minimum angular size "roughly 2.5° to 3° for both direct touch and ray casting"; 12 mm between targets at touch distance. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/styles_inputs_hit_targets/
16. **Target size, Android XR.** Target 56dp, visible part 48dp, 8dp between targets; text 14dp or more. At 0.868 dp per dmm that is about 2.8° (computed). https://developer.android.com/design/ui/xr/guides/visual-design
17. **Buttons.** One primary per view; others secondary or borderless; red only for delete or end-session; short verb labels ("Save", "Delete"). https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/buttons_bp/
18. **Button order.** Default action on the trailing side (or top of a stack), Cancel on the leading side (or bottom); a destructive choice always comes with Cancel; Cancel is never the default; at most three buttons. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts — visionOS puts close/back at top-left (WWDC23 10076).
19. **Scrolling is allowed but not by scrollbar.** Scroll from anywhere on the panel, cut content visibly at the edge as the cue, no scrollbar the user must hit; dropdowns should fit without scrolling. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/dropdowns/ — no nested same-direction scrolling; page-by-page is an option. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views
20. **Steps.** A multi-step task in a sheet gets one path with a Back button; never show Cancel, Done and Back together. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets — do not reveal extra actions on hover (layout shifts under the pointer). https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/
21. **Selectors.** Radio buttons stacked vertically with text on the right; toggle with its label on the left; discrete sliders show dots; a dropdown shows the current choice. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/selectors/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/sliders/

## 3. Form lifecycle and feedback
22. **A sheet is modal.** It floats in front of its parent and dims it; centre it in the field of view, do not slide it from the bottom edge, do not cover most of the parent, one sheet at a time. Cancel closes without saving; Done closes after saving; Done always comes with Cancel or Back. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets
23. **Modal only when it helps.** Always an obvious way out; ask before closing if the user would lose typed content. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality
24. **Non-modal for side information.** Content the user needs while still working goes in a split view or an adjacent window. (sheets and layout pages, URLs above and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
25. **Alerts.** In front of the window, slightly forward, anchored to it when it moves; in a full immersive space, centred in the field of view. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts
26. **Meta dialogs.** Temporary, blocking, "use them cautiously"; one primary action, an optional secondary (often Cancel), an optional progress part, one or several steps. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/dialogs/
27. **MRTK dialogs.** One or two buttons; closes on a button and returns the result. Far placement follows the user at 1.5–2.0 m (default 1.8 m), near at 0.3–0.9 m (default 0.7 m), constant apparent size; panels 192×96, 192×128, 192×192 mm. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/dialog-ui and https://github.com/microsoft/MixedRealityToolkit-Unity/blob/main/Assets/MRTK/SDK/Features/UX/Scripts/Dialog/Dialog.cs
28. **While working.** Show progress when it takes "more than a moment or two"; let people do other things. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading — every control needs hover, pressed and disabled looks. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/buttons_bp/
29. **Success.** Confirm only significant actions — people "expect their action or task to succeed, they only need to know when it doesn't"; put status next to the thing it describes. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback
30. **Errors.** Check values as they are entered, say why something cannot be done, enable Next only when required data is present. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data — mark the field, keep what was typed (2nd, NN/g URL above).
31. **Toasts in VR: no vendor number** for place or duration. Nearest facts: Meta puts captions at the top or bottom of a 40° field, about 1 m away. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/accessibility/ — Android 2D toasts last 2 s or 3.5 s (2nd). https://github.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/blob/master/services/core/java/com/android/server/notification/NotificationManagerService.java

## 4. Where a form panel appears, and how big
32. **Android XR.** Panel centre 1.75 m away and 5° below eye level; content inside the central 41°; allowed depth 0.75–5 m; apparent size constant between 0.75 and 1.75 m. The default 1024×720dp panel is 1556×1094 mm at 1.75 m, about 48°×35° (computed). https://developer.android.com/design/ui/xr/guides/spatial-ui and https://developer.android.com/design/ui/xr/guides/visual-design
33. **visionOS.** First window where the person is looking; a later window in front of an existing one with a small offset, or at a chosen edge of it. Default 1280×720 pt at "about two meters". Wider rather than taller; nothing "too high up or too far down". https://developer.apple.com/documentation/visionos/positioning-and-sizing-windows and https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows
34. **Beyond arm's reach.** Content "a bit further than arm's reach"; reading content "farther than arm's-length". https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10072/ and https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10078/
35. **Meta, ray-operated UI.** Comfortable 0.8–3 m; avoid 0.5–0.8 m (people try to touch it); never nearer than 0.5 m for things looked at long, 1 m is comfortable for menus. Keep the angular size when a panel moves in depth. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/display/
36. **Facing.** Panels turn toward the user in pitch and yaw, never roll; otherwise keep them level. (hands-ui URL above) — a visionOS volume may tilt toward a reclining viewer. (windows URL above)
37. **Stay put.** Panels stay in place when the user moves; anchor to the space or "loosely follow the user using smoothing animation"; never head-locked. https://developer.android.com/design/ui/xr/guides/spatial-ui and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/mr-design-guideline/ — re-centring is a system action on visionOS. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout
38. **MRTK follow defaults.** Follow: object kept within 30° horizontally and 20° vertically of the view, turns to face the user only past a 60° dead zone. RadialView: 1–2 m, 30° cone. ConstantViewSize: 0.5–3.5 m. https://github.com/microsoft/MixedRealityToolkit-Unity/tree/main/Assets/MRTK/SDK/Features/Utilities/Solvers
39. **Do not cover the thing being edited.** Supplementary content opens in an adjacent window; attached controls overlap the window's bottom edge by 20 pt (Apple, WWDC23 10076) or sit 20dp off the panel (Android XR, spatial-ui URL above); "avoid overlapping panels". Microsoft's object menu sits on the bottom edge of the object's bounds, on the side nearest the user. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout and https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/app-bar-and-bounding-box
40. **Panel size limits.** Meta: default 1024×640dp, minimum 384×500, maximum 1440×1000. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/styles_layouts/ — Android XR maximum 2560×1800dp. (spatial-ui URL above)

## 5. Spawning a new 3D object
41. **Android XR.** "3D models launch at 1.5 meters and 15 degrees below a user's line of sight, in the center of their field of view. They open in the miniature size of 1.5 meters per axis." https://developer.android.com/design/ui/xr/guides/3d-content
42. **Meta.** Place objects "at a roughly 1 meter distance slightly below the user's line of sight"; "avoid objects that suddenly appear too close or large"; fade them in; apply a minimum and maximum size (no numbers). https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/mr-design-guideline/
43. **Microsoft.** Best zone 1.25–5 m; interact at 1 m or more; never nearer than 40 cm; the closer, the worse ("increase exponentially"). https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/comfort
44. **MRTK tap-to-place.** Object goes to the surface the head or controller ray hits; with no hit it sits 1.5 m along the ray (default); ray searches up to 20 m. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/mrtk-unity/mrtk2/features/ux-building-blocks/solvers/tap-to-place (defaults in TapToPlace.cs, solvers URL above)
45. **visionOS.** A volume opens like a window (item 33), is sized in metres and keeps real scale; nearby objects "invite interaction", far ones can be large. (positioning URL above and WWDC23 10072)
46. **Angular size of a 0.5 m box** (computed, distance to its centre): 53° at 0.5 m, 28° at 1 m, 19° at 1.5 m, 14° at 2 m. At 0.5 m its near face is 0.25 m from the eyes — inside every vendor's near limit.
47. **Personal space (peer-reviewed, about people not objects).** Distance kept from an unfamiliar person "can vary widely across individuals, often averaging between 60 to 100 cm"; the study measured the same kind of discomfort-by-distance curve for VR avatars and real people. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-99578-1
48. **Gaze or ray hit?** No vendor states a rule. Apple uses "where they're looking"; MRTK uses whichever ray is configured (head by default) and falls back to a fixed distance.

## 6. Bounds and selection affordances
49. **Meaning.** A bounding box says "this object is currently adjustable" and shows its full extent. Corners scale; the marks at edge midpoints rotate. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/design/app-bar-and-bounding-box
50. **MRTK2 numbers.** Handles 1.6 cm with 1.6 cm extra grab padding; edge lines 1 mm radius; faces drawn only if a box material is set (none by default); white handles, blue when grabbed; move handles off by default. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/mrtk-unity/mrtk2/features/ux-building-blocks/bounds-control
51. **MRTK2 show/hide.** Shown at start, on hand proximity, on pointer hover, on either, or manually. With the proximity effect, handles are hidden when far, appear within 10 cm and grow 1.5× within 3 cm. (same URL; defaults in ProximityEffectConfiguration.cs)
52. **MRTK3 changed its mind.** Recommended style shows only the relevant handles along the edges, and the lines contract, thicken and change colour with intent. The old all-handles box is "visual noise" and not recommended. Clicking the object in place toggles handles; a no-handles variant exists as a pure intent cue; always add a min/max scale limit. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/mixed-reality/mrtk-unity/mrtk3-spatialmanipulation/packages/spatialmanipulation/bounds-control
53. **visionOS volume.** No visible frame; the floor edge glows gently when looked at; the resize control appears when looking at a corner; the bar and close button turn to face the viewer. https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows
54. **Meta.** Move handles appear on hovering an edge, resize handles on hovering a corner (resize from the centre). Grab preview: a translucent copy at 25% opacity with a solid white outline. Use different highlights for grabbable objects and clickable UI. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/windows/ and https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/grab_specs/
55. **Ultraleap.** Round nodes at each corner with arrows for resize; a small round handle at the base for moving (no sizes). https://docs.ultraleap.com/xr-guidelines/Components/ui-panels.html
56. **Blender.** "Bounds" just draws a bounding shape around the object — a passive display, no handles. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/scene_layout/object/properties/display.html
57. **No vendor publishes** an opacity for bounds faces or a handle size for ray input (1.6 cm is for fingers at arm's length).

## 7. Virtual keyboard and text fields
58. **visionOS.** The keyboard is its own window that people move where they want; "you don't need to account for the location of the keyboard in your layouts". Pick the keyboard type per field (numbers, URL). https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards
59. **Meta.** It appears in front of the user when a field is selected, nearer than the field, and shows the typed text above the keys so the eyes do not travel (not for passwords). It must not sit "too low out of the FOV". Has a dismiss key, suggestions and dictation. No distance, size or tilt numbers. https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/virtual-keyboard/
60. **Distance zone.** A ray-typed keyboard follows item 35: 0.8 m or more, never in the 0.5–0.8 m band.
61. **Alternatives.** Choices, defaults, suggestion chips, a microphone button. (items 11 and 59)

## What this implies for a ray-operated 2D form panel and for a box-shaped object marker
- Translucent panels are fine: keep the backplate at least half opaque (item 10), dark, and blur or darken what is behind it if possible.
- Inside it, make input fields darker and more opaque than the panel, buttons lighter; never put a translucent panel on another translucent panel.
- Use medium or bold text and check 4.5:1 contrast against the brightest scene the panel can float over.
- When a form opens from a panel, push that panel back and dim it; one form at a time.
- One column, label above each field, pickers and defaults before typing, everything visible without scrolling where possible.
- Rows and buttons: hit area 2.5–3° (about 65–80 mm at 1.5 m), visible gap between them; this is larger than the 1° floor in the earlier digest.
- One primary button on the trailing side, Cancel on the leading side and quieter, destructive in red and apart.
- Submit: disable and show progress, close on success and let the result in the world be the confirmation; on failure keep the form and put the message at the field. Cancel closes with no change.
- Open the form 1–1.75 m away, centre 5–15° below eye level, facing the user, fixed in the world, beside the edited object, with its content inside the central 41° (item 32).
- Keyboard: nearer and lower than the form but inside the view, shows the text being typed, appears on field select, has a dismiss key.
- New object: 1–1.5 m or more along the ray (hit point if any), never nearer than 0.5 m; a 0.5 m box spans 28° at 1 m and 19° at 1.5 m (no vendor gives a maximum angular size for a new object); fade it in.
- Box marker: passive = thin edges only, no faces; hover = edges brighten; edit = corner handles (scale) and edge-midpoint handles (rotate) shown on hover, coloured when grabbed, sized by angle for the ray.
